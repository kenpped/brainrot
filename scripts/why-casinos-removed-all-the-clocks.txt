Casinos didn't ban clocks because of a law or a conspiracy — they did it because time is the one thing that reminds you to leave. Every minute you don't check is a minute they keep your money. Here's the design behind it.

Fact one: Las Vegas's classic casino floors were shaped by Bugsy Siegel's Flamingo in 1946, which pioneered the windowless, clockless layout the Strip copied for decades. Fact two: casinos are among the only major businesses that run 24 hours a day, 365 days a year — there is no closing time to push you out. Fact three: on average, the longer you play, the more the house edge grinds you down; roulette's edge is about 5.26 percent per spin on an American wheel, and it never gets tired.

Here's the twist: it barely matters anymore. Everyone carries a phone with a clock. So casinos stopped hiding time and started competing with it — loyalty points, free drinks, comps timed to keep you seated.

The clock was never the enemy. Your attention was.
